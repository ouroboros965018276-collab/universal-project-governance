# RC19 v10 cost diagnosis

This diagnosis preserves the frozen v10 result: 32 completed CLI turns, 2,571,232 reported tokens, no unknown calls, treatment quality 16/16 and control quality 15/16. The strict net-benefit gate failed. Total tokens decreased 5.4389%; collector wall time increased 0.6230%, with both model point estimates above 1 and both wall-time confidence intervals crossing 1. No failed pair is excluded from that result.

The measured callback was dominated by the CLI span: 95.57% of control time and 95.50% of treatment time. Control/treatment CLI totals were 291.3248/292.9214 seconds; collector totals were 304.8366/306.7356 seconds. Adapter preparation was 12.1287/12.0745 seconds and grading was 0.5018/0.6715 seconds. The CLI span includes local process/host/tool startup and remote work; these records do not separate their contributions or measure provider API-request counts. Summed broker execution took 2.9064/3.0189 seconds, with overlapping tool calls, so those sums are not critical-path measurements.

Treatment used 28 reads, 24 patches, 16 validations and 19 status calls, compared with 31/23/17/20 for control. Its 28 reads comprise 16 initial batches, two intermediate reads before dependent state updates, and ten reads after the final patch for content inspection. Initial batches already included all explicitly required paths and the state file for recovery/current-truth tasks. In both intermediate cases, every checksum subsequently written to the state record was already available from the latest verified edit receipt or the initial read of unchanged content. Re-reading was unnecessary to obtain those checksums; the logs cannot establish that checksum retrieval was the model's only purpose. These reads also returned full content. A narrow instruction can therefore encourage direct checksum reuse while preserving semantic inspection, stale/missing evidence, failed-check and later-edit exceptions, and every required final check. Checksums establish byte identity, not semantic correctness.

Output verbosity was not the main observed difference: reported output tokens increased from 12,576 to 12,856, while final replies totaled 6,075/6,039 UTF-8 bytes. Input tokens decreased by 72,158. The current-truth family contributed 63,018 of the total 71,878-token reduction. Validations and status overlapped in 30 of 32 calls already. Observed MCP overlap groups decreased from 63 to 58; they are not provider API rounds. The records provide no per-event timestamps or tool-receipt wait breakdown, so queueing, inference, network, and host delay cannot be assigned causal shares.

The single failed control recovery call was 1.8073 seconds faster than its successful treatment, against a total treatment-minus-control difference of 1.8990 seconds. This explains a descriptive imbalance; it does not justify excluding that pair, relaxing quality, or claiming a wall-time improvement. Other pairs also show that fewer tokens or tool groups do not guarantee lower latency.

The v10 source/plan preparation cost, 6.9028 seconds for both conditions combined, and collector setup, 1.5682 seconds, occurred outside per-task timing and cannot be assigned to either condition after the fact. A5 measured a different native artifact callback that includes its preparation/cache path. ToolCodex does not invoke NativePreparer. A5's callback improvement therefore cannot be added to v10 or used to establish real-tool end-to-end savings. A future end-to-end claim requires a prospectively registered implementation and matched timing endpoints.

For this diagnosis, all 32 raw stdout and broker journal hashes matched their receipts; unique completed-turn usage matched the recorded input/output counts, and broker events matched frozen results. Existing quality outcomes were read without regrading.
